---
title: "Find opportunities"
description: "Turn your Search Console data into a ranked list of pages to improve, then review and approve them."
---

Opportunities turns your project's Search Console data into a review queue. It shows which existing pages deserve work, with the numbers behind each one.

Approving an opportunity only allows Relay to prepare a proposal. It does not change your website. To change a page, prepare a proposal and approve its pull request separately. See [Publish changes to GitHub](/docs/github-publishing).

## Get started

1. Select the project for your website.
2. Connect its Search Console property in **Project settings → Integrations**. See [Connect Google Search Console](/docs/google-search-console).
3. Open **Opportunities** and click **Run detection**.
4. Open a result to see the page, the metrics, the score and the uncertainty.
5. Click **Mark reviewed**. Then approve proposal preparation, reject the opportunity, or defer it for 7 days.

Detection needs no credits, no AI model and no GitHub connection. GA4 is optional. When it is connected, Relay uses it to score commercial value. Organization members can read the queue and run detection. Owners and administrators record review decisions.

## What Relay looks for

- **Striking distance:** the best page for a query has an average position from 5 to 20, with at least 100 impressions in the current window. This is a reason to look at the page and the search results. It is not an automatic request to rewrite the title.
- **Declining page:** the same URL is in both windows, had at least 30 clicks before, and lost at least 20 clicks and 30% of its clicks. A URL that is missing from a report is unknown, not zero.
- **Low click-through:** the page gets 60% or less of the clicks that pages on your own site get at the same positions. Relay builds this click-through curve from your data. For each position from 1 to 10 with at least 5 queries of 100 or more impressions, it takes the median click-through rate. A page qualifies when it has at least 500 non-branded impressions in positions 1 to 10 and is at least 20 clicks below the curve.
- **Competing pages:** two pages each get at least 25% of the impressions for the same non-branded queries, with at least 50 impressions and a position of 20 or better. The pair qualifies when it shares 2 or more queries, or the shared queries have 200 or more impressions. The page with more clicks is the primary page. Two addresses for the same page (for example with and without `www`) do not count. Competing pages can split clicks, but it is not always a problem.
- **Technical blocker:** the latest site audit, if it finished in the last 30 days, found a problem on a page that has at least 50 impressions in either window, or on a key page. Relay checks these problems: noindex, blocked by robots.txt, canonical to another page, conflicting canonicals, broken page, server error, redirect loop, redirect chain, content that needs JavaScript, missing title, duplicate title and slow response. Each result names the page, its impressions and one fix.
- **Internal link:** a page that ranks for one of the top 10 queries of a money or hub key page (position 30 or better) does not link to that key page yet. The source page must return status 200 and be indexable in the latest audit. Relay suggests up to 5 source pages for each key page, and it needs at least 3 non-branded queries for the key page. When almost every page already links to a key page, Relay suggests nothing for it.

### Why a page declined

For a declining page, Relay also reads the same two windows one year earlier (364 days back, so the weekdays match). It then gives one diagnosis:

| Diagnosis           | Rule                                                                                                   |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Possibly seasonal   | One year earlier, the page lost at least 70% as many clicks over the same weeks. Confidence goes down. |
| Ranking drop        | The average position became worse by 2 or more.                                                        |
| Click-through drop  | Impressions changed by less than 15%, and the click-through rate fell by 30% or more.                  |
| Demand drop         | Impressions fell by 30% or more, and the position changed by less than 1.                              |
| More than one cause | Two or more of the three rules above match.                                                            |
| Cause unclear       | No rule matches.                                                                                       |

The diagnosis is a rule of thumb from Search Console totals. It does not check page edits or the layout of the search results.

### Investigate a declining page

To look deeper, open a declining page and click **Investigate**. You need permission to manage integrations. Relay runs four checks and lists what it found, most severe first:

1. **Query mix:** the queries that lost the most clicks between the two periods of the analysis. Relay uses the same dates as the analysis, so you can compare the numbers in Search Console.
2. **Index state:** what URL Inspection says now: indexed or not, a robots.txt block, and a canonical that Google chose differently.
3. **Site audit:** the issues that the latest completed site audit found on the page.
4. **Rank history:** how your tracked keywords that are also the page's queries moved.

When a check cannot run, for example because the URL Inspection quota is used up, the list says so and the other checks still run. An investigation uses no credits.

Click **Summarize with AI** to get a short paragraph about the most likely cause. The model sees only the findings. Relay rejects a summary that adds a number or a claim that the findings do not state. A summary costs about 1 to 5 credits.

### Brand terms

Searches for your brand are not a sign of a weak page. Low click-through and competing pages leave out every query that contains a brand term as a whole word. Until you save your own terms, Relay uses the first part of your domain, for example `acme` for `acme.com`. Edit the terms at the bottom of the project **Context** page, or ask your AI client to update them. A project can have up to 20 terms.

### Run details

Each run compares two 28-day windows that end 3 days before today, in Google's Pacific reporting time. It reads up to 5,000 query rows and up to 1,000 page rows from each page report. It saves at most 50 opportunities.

The reports are samples, not a full export. Search Console can leave out anonymized queries. When a report reaches its row limit, Relay marks the coverage as limited and lowers its confidence. Relay does not check search intent or the layout of the search results.

When your data has no qualifying pages, Relay says so. It never invents opportunities to fill the queue.

### Site audits and URL inspection

Technical blockers and internal links read your latest site audit. When an audit finishes, Relay saves which pages link to each money or hub key page. Run a new audit after you add key pages or change links. When a run cannot use a check, its result says why, for example "No recent site audit".

For the top 10 technical blockers that can stop indexing, Relay asks Google's URL Inspection tool how Google sees the page. When Google confirms the problem, confidence goes up from 0.50 to 0.70, and the detail view shows Google's answer. URL inspection is free, but Google limits it, so each project can use at most 50 inspections a day.

When a striking-distance query is also a tracked keyword, the detail view shows its rank history over the last 30 days. Rank history does not change the score.

When the project has an AI visibility tracker, the detail view shows how many AI answers in the last 30 days cite the page. AI citations do not change the score.

### Content gaps (paid)

Content gaps find commercial topics where your competitors rank and your site barely appears. Click **Find content gaps** on the Opportunities page and pick up to 3 competitors from your project context. Relay shows the cost in credits before it buys anything. Nothing is bought until you confirm.

Relay buys each competitor's top-10 keywords with 50 or more monthly searches from DataForSEO. It keeps a keyword when its intent is commercial or transactional, your site has fewer than 10 impressions for it, and no tracked keyword of yours ranks in the top 20 for it. It groups the keywords into topics by their two most frequent words. A topic becomes an opportunity when it has 3 or more keywords, or 500 or more monthly searches.

Competitor data is saved for 30 days. A new run with the same competitors in that time is free. Content gaps can run once a day for each project. When your credits run out, Relay stops before the next purchase and keeps the data it already bought.

### Live results check (paid)

A low click-through page can have ads, an AI overview or a featured snippet above it. On a low click-through opportunity, click **Check live results**. Relay shows the cost first, then buys one live Google results page for the page's top query. When any of these features are above your page, confidence goes down by 0.15. When none are, it goes up by 0.10. The check is reused for 24 hours.

## How priority works

The score sets the review order. It is not a traffic, conversion or revenue forecast:

```text
priority = round(100 × confidence × (
  0.30 × commercial relevance
  + 0.25 × observed demand
  + 0.20 × visibility opportunity
  + 0.15 × (1 − difficulty)
  + 0.10 × inverse effort
))
```

The detail view shows every value and where it came from:

- **Commercial relevance** is the higher of two scores. The page role in your project context gives 1 for a money page, 0.7 for a hub, 0.5 for other roles and 0.25 for an unknown page. When GA4 is connected, organic key events on the page over the current window give 0 for none, 0.7 for 1 to 9, and 1 for 10 or more.
- **Demand** uses observed impressions.
- **Difficulty** uses the keyword difficulty that you already researched in Relay, when it is 90 days old or newer. Relay never buys new data for this. Otherwise, the average position stands in for difficulty.
- **Effort** is 2 hours for striking distance, 4 hours for a decline, 1 hour for low click-through, 4 hours for competing pages, 30 minutes for an internal link and 8 hours for a content gap. For a technical blocker, it is 1 to 8 hours by problem type.
- **Confidence** is a fixed rule of thumb for each type, not a probability of success.

## Review and history

Search by part of a URL or a query. Filter by status, type, owner, freshness or minimum priority. Sort by priority or by newest. The status list shows how many items each status has with your other filters. Every filter stays in the page address, so you can share a filtered list with your team.

A new detection run updates an existing opportunity. It does not make a copy. Each decision records who made it, the note and the evidence it was based on. When new evidence arrives, review it before you approve. You cannot approve evidence that is older than 14 days, or that belongs to a disconnected or changed Search Console property.

### Owners

Owners and administrators can give each opportunity to one member of the organization, or leave it unassigned. Approving an opportunity makes you its owner. Each change of owner appears in the review history. Use the **Owner** filter to see **Assigned to me**, unassigned items or one member's items.

### Defer

A deferred opportunity leaves the default queue for 7, 30 or 90 days. You choose the length when you defer. Turn on **Show deferred** to see it sooner. A deferred item shows the date when it comes back.

### Not in latest analysis

When the latest run did not find an opportunity again, it shows **Not in latest analysis**. The issue may be fixed, or the data may have changed. Relay keeps the item and every decision about it. When a later run finds it again, the label goes away. Content gaps count only content-gap runs for this label.

### Tickets

When an opportunity is approved, click **Create ticket** to get plain text for your team's tracker. The ticket has the pages, the evidence numbers, the proposed change, the risk and how to verify the result. Relay writes it from the stored evidence only, with no AI. It never includes review notes. With a GitHub connection, you can also create the same text as a GitHub issue. See [Publish changes to GitHub](/docs/github-publishing).

## Limits and failures

- A successful run is reused for 24 hours. Filtering the queue reads saved results only.
- Each run makes at most 5 Search Console requests, 1 GA4 request and 10 URL inspections. If the GA4 request or an inspection fails, the run continues without it.
- One run at a time can work on a project. A failed run can be retried after 5 minutes.
- When Google is unavailable, the existing opportunities stay. A run that fails partway publishes nothing new.

Detection makes no paid calls. Only content gaps and live results checks use credits, and both show the cost before you confirm.
